Best Cement for House Construction in Kenya

Choosing the right Simba Cement depends on the stage of your house construction project. Structural concrete and general masonry have different requirements.

Which Simba Cement Is Best for House Construction in Kenya?

The right Simba Cement grade depends on the building phase.

Use Simba Cement 42.5N for demanding structural elements such as foundations, columns and reinforced concrete slabs where the structural specification requires it.

Use Simba Cement 32.5R for general construction activities such as masonry, walling, block work, screeding and plastering.

Simba Cement is produced by National Cement Company Limited in Kenya and is used across residential and commercial construction projects.

A house contains several types of construction work. The same cement grade does not need to be selected for every stage.

Structural concrete needs should follow the structural engineer's drawings and concrete specification, while general walling and finishing work can use cement suited to masonry and plastering.

Simba Cement 42.5N for Structural House Construction

Simba Cement 42.5N is the higher-grade option for demanding structural concrete applications.

Foundations

Use the specified structural cement and concrete class for strip footings, pad foundations, foundation slabs and reinforced foundation work.

Structural Columns

42.5N is suited to reinforced concrete columns and other load-bearing structural elements where specified.

Concrete Slabs

Structural floor slabs and roof slabs require concrete produced according to the project's structural specification.

Beams

Structural beams carry substantial loads and should use the specified concrete class and cement grade.

Why Use 42.5N for Structural Work?

Simba Cement 42.5N is a higher-strength grade intended for demanding concrete applications where structural performance matters.

It is therefore the stronger choice for core structural areas such as foundations, columns, beams and slabs when the design calls for this cement grade.

Simba Cement 32.5R for Masonry, Walling and Plastering

Simba Cement 32.5R is a versatile cement grade suited to common general construction and finishing activities.

In cement strength-class terminology, the letter "R" indicates rapid early strength development.

Typical house-building applications include:

  • Stone masonry
  • Block walling
  • Brick laying
  • Mortar
  • Plastering
  • Floor screeding
  • General non-specialized construction

Workability for General Construction

General construction cement should allow builders to produce workable mortar and plaster mixes when materials, water proportions and workmanship are properly controlled.

Good surface finishing also depends on correct sand quality, water control, mixing, application and curing.

Which Simba Cement Should You Use at Each Stage of House Construction?

Foundation Construction

Use Simba Cement 42.5N where the structural specification requires a higher-strength cement for foundation concrete.

Ground Beams and Structural Columns

Use the engineer-specified cement and concrete class. Simba Cement 42.5N is suited to demanding reinforced concrete work.

Walling

Simba Cement 32.5R is suitable for stone masonry, block laying and general wall construction.

Concrete Slabs

Structural floor or roof slabs should follow the project's concrete design. Simba Cement 42.5N is suited to demanding slab work where specified.

Plastering

Simba Cement 32.5R is suited to general plastering applications when mixed and applied correctly.

Floor Screeding

Simba Cement 32.5R can be used for general screeding applications subject to the required floor specification.

Practical Tips When Using Simba Cement for House Construction

1. Follow the Structural Design

Structural elements such as foundations, beams, columns and slabs should follow the engineer's concrete specification.

2. Control the Water

Avoid adding excessive water to concrete, mortar or plaster mixes. Water content influences workability and final quality.

3. Use Clean Sand and Aggregates

Construction materials should be appropriate for their intended application and free from excessive dirt and organic contamination.

4. Mix Materials Consistently

Measure materials consistently and mix them thoroughly before placement or application.

Read our Concrete Mixing Ratios guide →

5. Cure Concrete and Plaster Correctly

Maintain suitable curing conditions after concrete placement and plaster application.

A common site practice is to maintain moisture for at least seven days for many concrete and plaster applications, but the required curing method and duration should follow the project specification and construction conditions.

6. Store Cement Properly

Keep 50kg cement bags elevated above the floor on wooden pallets or another suitable raised platform.

Protect cement from rain, leaking roofs, ground moisture and damp walls.

How Many Cement Bags Do You Need for a House?

The quantity depends on the house design, floor area, foundation type, number of floors, wall material, slab design, plastering area and concrete requirements.

Divide the project into:

  • Foundation concrete
  • Ground beams
  • Columns
  • Floor slabs
  • Roof slabs where applicable
  • Stone or block masonry
  • Plastering
  • Floor screeding
  • Other concrete works

Calculate each section separately before combining the quantities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does R mean in Simba Cement 32.5R?
