Simba Cement Site Delivery Checklist Kenya

Use this checklist to verify quantities, inspect bag condition, confirm delivery documents, supervise offloading and protect Simba Cement from moisture after it reaches your construction site.

Why Use a Cement Delivery Checklist?

Do not wait until the delivery truck has left to discover missing bags, damaged packaging or moisture-affected cement.

A site delivery checklist gives one person responsibility for checking the order as it arrives.

Before accepting a Simba Cement delivery, compare the physical shipment against the order, check the product grade, inspect packaging and supervise placement into dry storage.

These checks become more important for bulk cement deliveries because a single truck can contain many bags and damaged stock becomes harder to identify after stacking.

Pre-Delivery Site Checklist

Prepare the site before the delivery vehicle arrives.

  • Confirm the expected delivery date or window
  • Confirm the ordered number of bags
  • Confirm the Simba Cement grade ordered
  • Prepare the delivery contact person
  • Ensure the access road is usable
  • Clear the truck turning and offloading area
  • Remove mud or standing water from the unloading zone
  • Prepare raised pallets before arrival
  • Prepare dry covered storage space
  • Ensure workers are available for safe offloading
Site access: Large delivery vehicles need enough road width, gate clearance and turning space. Check access before dispatch rather than when the truck reaches the site.

Checks When the Cement Truck Arrives

Check the Truck

Inspect the general condition of the load and confirm that the cement remained protected during transport.

Check the Cover

If the truck uses a tarpaulin or similar weather cover, confirm that it protected the bags from rain and water exposure.

Check the Product

Confirm that the delivered cement grade matches what appears on your order or delivery documentation.

Do not begin rapid offloading before the delivery paperwork and initial truck load have been checked.

Verify Delivery Documentation

Compare the delivery documents with what is physically on the truck.

  • Customer or project name
  • Delivery site
  • Order number where applicable
  • Product description
  • Cement grade
  • Quantity dispatched
  • Delivery date
  • Vehicle or dispatch details where provided

Check the Product Type

Confirm whether the delivered product matches the type and grade you ordered.

For example, a project may require Simba Cement 32.5R for masonry or plastering and Simba Cement 42.5N for demanding structural concrete.

Request Appropriate Documentation

Where your project requires product quality or compliance documentation, request the applicable official information from the supplier or manufacturer.

Keep delivery notes, invoices and other order documents together for project records.

Cement Quality Inspection Checklist

Inspect bags while they are still easy to separate from the rest of the shipment.

Check for Wet Bags

Look for visible water marks, damp packaging or bags that feel abnormally wet.

Check for Hard Lumps

Significant hard lumps can indicate moisture exposure and partial hydration inside the bag.

Check for Torn Packaging

Identify badly torn bags and bags that are losing substantial quantities of cement powder.

Check Product Markings

Confirm that branding, cement grade and other visible product markings are legible.

Separate Questionable Bags

Do not bury damaged or suspicious bags in the middle of the storage stack.

Document problems before the truck leaves.

If you identify missing, torn, wet or seriously damaged bags, record the issue during the receiving process and follow the supplier's delivery-resolution procedure.

Count Every Cement Bag

Compare the number of bags received against the delivery note or waybill.

For a large order, use a simple tally system.

One worker can supervise unloading while another records groups of bags as they move from the truck to storage.

Counting tip: Count during offloading instead of trying to count a tightly packed storage stack after unloading has finished.

Safe Cement Offloading Procedure

The offloading route should be short, dry and clear.

Clear the Pathway

Remove obstacles, standing water, mud and loose debris between the delivery truck and storage area.

Avoid Throwing Cement Bags

Rough handling can tear paper packaging and create cement losses.

Move Bags Directly to Storage

Avoid unloading cement onto bare soil first and planning to move it later.

Keep Bags Dry During Offloading

Stop or protect the offloading operation if rain creates a risk of exposing bags to water.

Truck safety: Keep unnecessary people away from the vehicle movement area and use a clear person to guide the driver where site space is limited.

Cement Storage Protocol After Delivery

Use Raised Pallets

Keep cement bags above the floor using wooden pallets or another suitable raised platform.

Use Dry Shelter

Store cement inside a dry, enclosed and weather-protected space.

Avoid Damp Walls

Leave separation between cement stacks and walls that can transfer moisture.

Protect from Rain

Check roofs, doors and openings for potential water entry before stacking cement.

Cover Where Needed

Additional waterproof polyethylene sheeting or a suitable tarp can provide extra protection when correctly installed.

Stack Safely

Keep stacks stable and avoid unsafe excessive heights that increase the risk of bag damage or collapse.

Do not place the bottom row directly on a concrete floor simply because the floor looks dry.

Floors can transmit moisture. Raised storage creates a useful separation between cement bags and the floor surface.

Use First-In, First-Out Stock Rotation

Arrange your storage so older suitable cement is accessible before newer deliveries.

This first-in, first-out approach reduces the chance of bags remaining unused for unnecessarily long periods.

Avoid planning around a rigid assumption that all cement remains suitable for exactly three months regardless of storage conditions.

Cement condition depends heavily on moisture protection, packaging integrity, storage environment and product guidance.

Stock-control tip: Mark or separate different delivery batches so workers know which suitable stock arrived first.

Planning Future Cement Deliveries

Large projects often perform better with planned deliveries aligned to actual construction progress.

Staged deliveries can reduce storage pressure and limit the amount of cement sitting unused on site.

When ordering, provide:

  • Cement grade
  • Number of bags
  • Delivery town
  • Exact site location
  • Access information
  • Preferred delivery schedule

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I check when Simba Cement arrives?

Check the delivery note, product grade, number of bags, packaging condition, signs of moisture exposure and general condition of the shipment before completing acceptance.

Should I count cement bags before signing the delivery note?

Count the delivered quantity and compare it with the documentation during the receiving process.

What do hard lumps inside a cement bag mean?

Significant hard lumps can indicate moisture exposure and partial hydration. Separate questionable bags for assessment rather than mixing them automatically with normal stock.

Should wet cement bags be accepted?

Visibly wet or moisture-damaged bags should be separated and documented during delivery inspection so the issue can be handled through the supplier's process.

How should cement be stored after delivery?

Store it in a dry waterproof shelter, raised on pallets, away from damp walls and protected from rain and humidity.

Should cement bags be stacked directly on concrete?

Raised pallets or another suitable platform are preferable because they separate the bags from floor moisture.

What does first-in, first-out mean for cement?

It means organizing stock so the older suitable cement delivery is used before newer stock.

How high should cement bags be stacked?

Use stable, safe stacks suited to the storage area and handling method. Avoid excessive heights that increase the risk of collapsing stacks or damaged bags.
